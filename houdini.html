Choose your first familiar thought or impulse, even if you went on to handle the situation differently. Choose what you recognise, not what you wish you did.

None of the four answers will fit perfectly. Choose the closest one. When two fit, choose the one that happens more often — especially when the moment feels personal.

Familiar is more useful than flattering. There is no good result and no bad result. This is a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict about who you are.

How these numbers are worked out

100 × the points your answers earned for a lens ÷ every point assigned to that lens. The denominator counts every answer option in all twenty questions, including the ones you did not choose.

The eight scores are independent — they are not meant to add up to 100. Where two competing answers in the same question both carry points for one lens, that lens cannot reach 100.

Rankings use the unrounded values and exact ties share a rank.
